Mystery Horse

A wordless, music-driven film

Following a young man and a towering, uncanny creature called The Horse — a story carried entirely by score and song, built for the dark of a theater.

40 minutes No dialogue Two characters Theatrical mix $100k–250k
01

Overview

Music comes first. The film is built to visually expand what the score already suggests, not the other way around — and it's mixed for impact in a theater, not for streaming, social, or standalone release.

  • 40 minutes, no dialogue — the story is carried entirely by score and songs
  • Two on-screen characters: the young man, and The Horse
  • Budget: $100k–$250k for production and marketing of the music/film, sourced through local crowdfunding

Tonal touchstones: Into the Wild, grounded, crossed with the mystery and mythic register of Pan's Labyrinth, Inception, and Beasts of the Southern Wild. How far to lean into the mythic side vs. the grounded side ties into the abstraction question in §8.

02

The Symbolic System

This is the spine the rest of the project hangs off, so it's stated once here rather than pieced together from three separate mentions — the Horse's description, a standalone symbolism note, and an orphaned "Whole / Fragment / Channel" line under Themes, which maps directly onto this triad and is folded in below.

Whether the audience consciously arrives at this reading is meant to stay ambiguous — mirroring the real uncertainty we have about our own "true" self.

03

Characters

Ego — the fragment

The Human

Young male lead. Still to be developed — background, arc, and specific turning points remain open (§8).

What's established so far: the story centers on raw introspection, disturbing realizations, and an ultimate action or revelation.

Self — the whole

The Horse

  • 12 feet tall, humanoid — not equine in any way; "Horse" is a title, not a description
  • Limbless, cloak-like, made of thousands of dark, thread-like structures
  • Never moves or walks — it spawns and stays stationary
  • Only the threads move: ambient drift → a single thread extending rapidly → shape-shifting → illumination

What the threads represent is still undecided — the single biggest open symbolic question in the project (§8).

Anima — the channel

The Voice

A soft female voice, disembodied, speaking in second person ("you") throughout the score.

Several songs shift perspective mid-track — verse to chorus, sometimes moving entirely into second person — to enact the ego → anima shift musically (§7).

04

Themes

  • Inaction and hesitation as quiet manifestations of doubt
  • Fear of commitment as the death of possibility
  • Fear of time passing, and regret
  • Retreat from the burden of expectation and productivity
  • Acceptance: however broken and imperfect the world is, it must be accepted as reality — the line that leads into the Whole / Fragment / Channel triad in §2
05

Intended Audience Effect

Curiosity and anticipation — the audience should keep leaning forward, unsure what The Horse is, what it wants, and what it means to the human.

07

Cross-Element Patterns

A few things that aren't stated outright but show up once the elements are lined up side by side.

1

Vocal perspective mirrors the Self/ego/anima system. Male-verse → female-chorus tracks (Soon Forgotten Dream, The Trees) enact the ego→anima handoff from §2 within a single song. Tracks that stay in the female voice throughout read as full excursions into the anima/Voice perspective rather than the grounded human one.

2

Percussion style tracks tempo treatment, not just mood. The rubato tracks either skip drums entirely or use only the sparse CBD device — never the fuller PERC kit. The tempo-locked/beat-driven tracks are the only ones using it. Worth deciding if that's a rule to keep applying as Instrumentals 3–5 get written.

3

A "modulate up a fifth" gesture marks two of the score's most pivotal moments — the final chorus of The Trees and the triumphant outro of TBD Song 7, the film's designated revelation track. Both also drop the bass an octave at the same moment. A strong candidate for a deliberate "transcendence" motif.

4

Tracks 8 and 9 currently have identical instrumentation — strings, slide guitar, flute — distinguished only by title, "Character" vs. "Action." Nothing in the arrangement notes marks that difference yet.

5

Trumpet calls form a loose, inconsistent motif. Confirmed in Nothing Is Strange, marked "possible" in Pleasant Valley and Endless Ocean, used most aggressively in TBD Song 7. Formalizing where it does and doesn't appear could do quiet narrative work for free.
